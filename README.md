This project focuses on enhancing atmospheric water generation through condensation on superhydrophobic surfaces. A vapour-compression atmospheric water generator and a solar-powered Peltier-based system were designed and studied using hydrophobic copper tubes and aluminium plates coated with PFOCTS. Experiments conducted in Noida under hot and humid conditions evaluated water production, power consumption, energy/exergy performance and environmental effects, while CFD simulations analysed airflow and condensation in the Peltier system. The study found that superhydrophobic surfaces promote efficient dropwise condensation, improving water-harvesting performance. The vapour-compression system produced up to about 10 L/h under favourable conditions, demonstrating the potential of atmospheric water generation as a sustainable solution to water scarcity.
